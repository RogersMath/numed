01 · The Market Context

A Growing Need — and a Broken Pricing Model

Demand for advanced wound care products is rising every year. The model that delivers them to buyers hasn't kept pace.

Chronic wounds — diabetic ulcers, pressure injuries, venous leg ulcers, surgical wounds — are among the most prevalent and costly challenges in modern healthcare. As the U.S. population ages and diabetes rates climb, wound care has become a defining operational and financial concern for hospitals, skilled nursing facilities, home health agencies, and physician practices alike.

The global advanced wound care market reached $13.6 billion in 2024 and is projected to exceed $19 billion by 2032.[1] Diabetic patients face a 15–25% lifetime risk of developing foot ulcers,[2] and 80% of lower-extremity amputations are preceded by these wounds.[3] The clinical demand is not diminishing.

The dominant distribution model in medical supply — Group Purchasing Organizations (GPOs) — was designed to lower costs for healthcare institutions. In practice, GPO vendors pay a percentage of revenue back to the GPO as "administrative fees." Since those fees are calculated on gross revenue, there is a structural incentive to keep prices high. The result: inflated list prices, dramatic-sounding discounts, and buyers paying more than they should for products in established clinical categories.[4]

"The current fee structure raises an obvious conflict of interest: since vendors pay the fees as a percentage of the product cost, the higher the price, the higher the GPOs' fees."[4]

Research published by the Medical Device Manufacturers Association found that when GPO-negotiated prices were exposed to open competition, hospitals achieved average savings of 10–14% across thousands of transactions — savings that had been invisible inside the GPO model.[5] NuMed Industries was built on the premise that healthcare buyers deserve a straightforward alternative.
